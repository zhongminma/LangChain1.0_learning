Aspirin is a commonly used medication for pain relief, fever reduction,
and inflammation control. It belongs to a group of drugs known as
nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs).

Common side effects of aspirin include stomach discomfort, nausea,
heartburn, and mild indigestion. These symptoms are more likely to occur
when aspirin is taken on an empty stomach.

More serious side effects may include gastrointestinal bleeding,
stomach ulcers, and allergic reactions such as rash, swelling, or
difficulty breathing. Long-term or high-dose use increases the risk
of these complications.

Aspirin can also increase the risk of bleeding because it interferes
with platelet function. Patients who are taking blood thinners or who
have bleeding disorders should consult a doctor before using aspirin.

In children and teenagers, aspirin use has been associated with
Reye’s syndrome, a rare but serious condition that affects the liver
and brain. For this reason, aspirin is generally not recommended for
children with viral infections.

Patients should always follow dosage instructions and seek medical
advice if unusual symptoms occur.
